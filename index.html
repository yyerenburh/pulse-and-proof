          How the lab works

          1. Pick a crew member in the list on the left. Most tests target the selected person.
          2. Press a button. Mission events change the simulated sensor signals to show how the crew is guided. Attacks play one threat from our threat model against the system.
          3. Watch the result in the lab log below: what was done, how long the system took to catch it, and how. Nothing here touches real data.

          Mission events

          • Selected person: temperature and heart rate rise over about 20 s.
          • Whole crew: blood oxygen falls, breathing speeds up.
          • Whole crew: dosimeters jump to more than 10 times the measured background.
          • Mars only: no contact with Earth, as for two weeks every two years.
          • Resets daily tasks and updates streaks.
          • Ends every simulated event.

          Checks in Pulse & Proof

            Attacks

            • A broken or hacked sensor keeps reporting the same normal value while the real one climbs.
            • An unregistered wearable sends readings for this person.
            • Readings use this person's device name but the wrong key.
            • An old, genuinely signed reading is sent again.
            • Someone with storage access lowers a past heart rate to hide a trend.
            • Space radiation flips one bit in an encrypted note.
            • One number in a signed report is changed after signing.
            • The astronaut tries to silence their own alerts.
            • Mission operations tries to read private notes.
            • Someone with the tablet tries PINs for the medical officer.
            • No contact with Earth. Press again to restore.
            • The next data bundle to Earth is altered on the way.
            • A fake "raise the heart-rate limit" command arrives from Earth.
